# Washington general election · November 3, 2026

Research refreshed October 28, 2026 using official sources, without Firecrawl. This consolidates the earlier research; it gives no voting recommendation. A YES enacts each initiative; a NO rejects that initiative and leaves existing law in place. All three appear in the [official VoteWA general-election guide](https://voter.votewa.gov/genericvoterguide.aspx?c=30&e=899) and [SOS measure materials](https://www.sos.wa.gov/so/node/12667).

Evidence labels: **Law / proposal** describes enacted or proposed text; **Official estimate** is a government forecast, not a guaranteed outcome; **Campaign argument** represents the advocates' position; **Interpretation / uncertainty** identifies a legal distinction or unresolved implementation detail.

## IP26-645 · Income-tax repeal

**At a glance:** Repeals the planned 9.9% tax on Washington taxable income above $1 million and prohibits state/local taxes on individual income or measured by individual income. [Attorney General explanation](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ExStmt_IP26-645.pdf)

| If YES                                                                                               | If NO                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Repeals the tax before collections begin; adopts the broader income-tax prohibition and definitions. | Keeps the 2026 tax law, scheduled to tax 2028 income, with first payments in 2029. |

**Brief history — law / proposal**

- Legislature enacted ESSB 6346 in March 2026. Governor signed March 30. [Enacted law](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/Senate/6346-S.SL.htm)
- Initiative certified July 22, 2026. [House nonpartisan summary](https://leg.wa.gov/media/ucubvqsd/2026-summary-of-initiative-26-645.pdf)
- Married couples and registered domestic partners share the $1 million deduction. The rate applies to taxable income above the deduction, rather than all income. [AG explanation](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ExStmt_IP26-645.pdf)

**Where the money goes — law**

- Five percent transfers to Fair Start for Kids for childcare and early learning beginning July 1, 2029; the remainder goes to the state general fund. [ESSB 6346 §202](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/Senate/6346-S.SL.htm)
- The general fund supports education, healthcare and other services; the statute does not assign a fixed education percentage or return the money to the taxpayer's district. Legislative intent to provide free school meals is not a specific appropriation. [ESSB 6346 §§1, 202](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Bills/Session%20Laws/Senate/6346-S.SL.htm)

**Fiscal effect — official estimate**

- OFM estimates **$11.432 billion less revenue over state fiscal years 2027–2031**, including $377 million less Fair Start transfers. It estimates **$150.47 million in net agency savings** over those five years. These are the official July 2026 ballot-statement estimates, with stated compliance and forecasting assumptions. [OFM fiscal statement](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/FIS%20-%20Initiative%20645%20-%20FINAL.pdf)

**Side by side — campaign arguments**

| Supporters say YES                                                                                                                                                    | Opponents say NO                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Avoid a new tax, protect investment, and prevent future expansion to people below the current threshold. They question whether lawmakers will keep spending promises. | Retain revenue for schools, healthcare and childcare, and ask high earners to contribute more to a tax system they regard as unfair to working families. |

These are summaries of the [campaign-authored pamphlet arguments](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/IP26-645%20VP%20page%209.17.pdf), not state findings.

**Claims to read carefully — interpretation / uncertainty**

- The opponents' pamphlet uses a $13 billion figure; the official OFM ballot fiscal statement uses $11.432 billion and a specified five-year period. Do not merge figures with different forecast dates or time windows.
- The AG says accompanying **non-income-tax credits, exemptions and deductions remain** after repeal. Claims that this vote itself automatically repeals the diaper exemption or Working Families Tax Credit conflict with that explanation; later legislative changes are a separate possibility. [AG explanation](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ExStmt_IP26-645.pdf)
- Forecasted service cuts depend on later budget decisions. Neither assured economic growth from repeal nor a specific classroom cut is established by this initiative's text.

## IL26-001 · Parental rights in public schools

**At a glance:** Restores the 2024 Initiative 2081 version of the parental-rights list in RCW 28A.605.005, reversing its 2025 revisions. It does **not** repeal all of HB 1296. [Full initiative](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Initiatives/Initiatives/INITIATIVE%20IL26-001.htm)

| If YES                                                                                                                          | If NO                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Restores the 2024 parental-rights list, including broader school-record access, a shorter deadline and medical-service notices. | Keeps the revised 2025 list and its existing records, notification and privacy provisions. |

**Brief history — law / proposal**

- 2024: Legislature enacted Initiative 2081.
- 2025: HB 1296 revised the list, aligned education-record provisions with federal law and expanded some protections.
- 2026: Legislature took no action on IL26-001, sending it to the general election. [House nonpartisan summary](https://leg.wa.gov/media/sw0bmjtf/2026-summary-of-initiative-26-001.pdf)

**Current law → proposed change**

| Topic                 | Current law                                                                                                                       | If initiative passes                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| School records        | Education-record access within a reasonable period, no more than 45 days; health-information disclosure governed by separate law. | 10 business days; free electronic copies; broader student-specific files, including health/counseling records.                               |
| Medical notices       | The 2025 list removed the prior general medical-service notices.                                                                  | Prior notice when services are offered except emergencies; notice of insurance/cost effects and school-arranged follow-up care.              |
| Sensitive activities  | Survey opt-outs follow federal categories; sex-education opt-out remains.                                                         | Restores broader language covering specified assignments and other activities.                                                               |
| Abuse-related records | Broader withholding provisions cover specified records, investigations and criminal proceedings.                                  | Restores the narrower health/mental-health exception while a parent is the target of an abuse/neglect investigation, subject to court order. |

Sources: [Current RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=28A.605.005), [AG explanation](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ExStat_IL26-001.pdf), [proposal](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Initiatives/Initiatives/INITIATIVE%20IL26-001.htm).

**Side by side — campaign arguments**

| Supporters say YES                                                                                                                      | Opponents say NO                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Parents need timely records and medical notices to guide their children's upbringing; broader communication builds family/school trust. | Broader records disclosure and narrower abuse exceptions could expose vulnerable students and discourage counseling; changed notification language could delay notice. |

These are summaries of the [campaign-authored pamphlet arguments](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IL26-001%20VP%20page.pdf).

**Fiscal effect — official estimate:** No state fiscal impact or revenue change; local policy/procedure costs may be minor but are indeterminate. [OFM](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/Initiative%20Measure%20NoIL26-001_FIS_FINAL.pdf)

**Distinctions that matter — interpretation / uncertainty**

- Parents already have rights to review materials, observe classrooms under applicable rules and opt out of comprehensive sex education. This vote changes an existing framework. [Current RCW](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=28A.605.005)
- Removing a right's repetition from this list does not repeal the separate statute protecting it. In particular, RCW 28A.320.160 independently requires immediate notice of alleged employee/contractor abuse or misconduct. [Separate notification statute](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=28A.320.160)
- Teacher antiretaliation protections are in another statute and are not repealed by this initiative. [RCW 28A.400.312](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=28A.400.312)
- No clause explicitly orders schools to report pronoun or gender-identity changes. Broader records access may disclose sensitive information; interaction with separate privacy/minor-consent laws is consequential. Neither “mandatory outing” nor “zero privacy risk” is an adequate legal summary.

## IL26-638 · Eligibility for girls' school-sports competitions

**At a glance:** Requires covered school-sports rules to exclude students the measure calls “biologically male” from female-only competitions and requires provider-signed sex verification for students seeking the female-only category. [Full initiative §§2–4](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Initiatives/Initiatives/INITIATIVE%20IL26-638.htm)

| If YES                                                                                                                                       | If NO                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adopts the eligibility restriction and medical-verification requirement. This generally excludes transgender girls from girls' competitions. | Keeps current state law and WIAA rules, which allow participation consistent with gender identity. |

**Brief history — law / proposal**

- Current WIAA rule 18.16.0 permits participation consistent with a student's gender identity. [2026–27 handbook, printed page 42](https://wiaa-dna4aga5arc0gyeb.westus2-01.azurewebsites.net/results/handbook/26-27/26-27%20Handbook.pdf)
- Filed in 2025 as an initiative to the Legislature.
- Legislature took no action in 2026, sending it to the November ballot. [House nonpartisan summary](https://leg.wa.gov/media/rtyf5u54/2026-summary-of-initiative-26-638.pdf)

**What the proposal actually requires — proposal**

- Covers female-only individual/team interschool competitions with separate male/female classifications.
- Each student entering the covered female category must supply a healthcare-provider statement as part of the sports physical, relying on reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup or normal endogenous testosterone levels.
- It specifies **no particular examination, laboratory test or numerical hormone threshold**. It does not itself impose a general college/recreational/PE/practice ban. [Full text](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Htm/Initiatives/Initiatives/INITIATIVE%20IL26-638.htm)
- Scope includes public K–12 schools and private schools participating through the covered interschool athletic organizations. [House summary](https://leg.wa.gov/media/rtyf5u54/2026-summary-of-initiative-26-638.pdf)

**Side by side — campaign arguments**

| Supporters say YES                                                                                                                | Opponents say NO                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sex-based eligibility protects fairness, safety, awards and opportunities for female athletes; verification can use routine care. | Excluding transgender girls harms inclusion, while requiring all female-category athletes to certify sex adds privacy, access and participation barriers. |

These are summaries of the [campaign-authored pamphlet arguments](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/IL26-638%20VP%20page.pdf).

**Fiscal effect — official estimate:** Approximately $21,000 one-time state guidance-update cost; local policy/rule costs indeterminate; no state/local revenue effect. This does not quantify every family's possible compliance cost. [OFM](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/Initiative%20Measure%20NoIL26-638_FIS_FINAL.pdf)

**Distinctions that matter — interpretation / uncertainty**

- The text mandates verification but leaves implementation unsettled. Claims that it definitely mandates a genital examination, or that existing records will always suffice without additional examination/testing, go beyond its explicit terms.
- Athlete counts, competitive outcomes, likely medical procedures and participation effects in campaign arguments require independent evidence; do not display them as government findings.
- The restriction concerns eligibility for the girls' category; effects on a particular student's practical opportunity to play require sport/school context.
